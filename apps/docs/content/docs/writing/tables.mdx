## A table

```latex
\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Convergence of the projection scheme}
  \label{tab:convergence}
  \begin{tabular}{lrr}
    \hline
    Mesh  & Error           & Rate \\
    \hline
    $h$   & $2.1\times10^{-3}$ & --   \\
    $h/2$ & $5.3\times10^{-4}$ & 1.99 \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}
```

`tabular` takes LaTeX's column types `l`, `c`, `r` and `p{width}`, `|` for vertical rules, and `\hline`, `\cline{2-3}` and `\multicolumn{2}{c}{...}`. The `table` float places it; see [Pictures and Floats](/docs/writing/pictures) for how floats are positioned.

## Rules worth reading

`booktabs`' rules are drawn with the weights and spacing booktabs gives them, heavy at the top and bottom and light between:

```latex
\usepackage{booktabs}

\begin{tabular}{lr}
  \toprule
  Name & Value \\
  \midrule
  Mass & 2.3   \\
  \cmidrule(lr){1-2}
  Charge & 1.6 \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
```

## Column types

`array`'s additions are read by `tabular` itself: `m{width}` and `b{width}` columns, `>{...}` and `<{...}` around a column, `@{...}` and `!{...}`, and column types of your own:

```latex
\usepackage{array}
\newcolumntype{C}[1]{>{\centering\arraybackslash}p{#1}}
\begin{tabular}{C{3cm}C{3cm}} ... \end{tabular}
```

## Tables to a width

* `tabularx`: `\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{lX}`, its `X` columns sharing what the others leave.
* `tabulary`: columns `L`, `C`, `R` and `J` share the width by how much text they hold.
* `tabu`: `\begin{tabu} to \linewidth {X[l] X[c] X[r]}`.
* tabularray's `tblr`, with `colspec=`, is read as the tabular it describes.

## Tables over several pages

`longtable` is set a row at a time, so a page can end between any two rows. The rows before `\endhead` repeat at the top of every page the table continues on, the rows before `\endfoot` close every page it breaks off, and `\endfirsthead` and `\endlastfoot` give the first and last pages their own:

```latex
\usepackage{longtable}

\begin{longtable}{lr}
  \caption{Prices} \\
  \hline Item & Price \\ \hline
  \endfirsthead
  \hline Item & Price \\ \hline
  \endhead
  \hline
  \endfoot
  Tea  & 2 \\
  Cake & 3 \\
\end{longtable}
```

`longtable*`, `supertabular`, `xtabular`, `xltabular`, tabularray's `longtblr` and tabu's `longtabu` break the same way.

## Cells

| Package          | What it does                                                                                                                |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `multirow`       | `\multirow{2}{*}{Text}` stands in the middle of the rows it spans, or level with the first for `[t]` and the last for `[b]` |
| `makecell`       | `\makecell{Two\\lines}` in one cell, and `\thead` for a bold head                                                           |
| `diagbox`        | A cell split by a diagonal, a label either side                                                                             |
| `colortbl`       | `\rowcolor`, `\cellcolor` and `\columncolor`; rules stay black                                                              |
| `threeparttable` | A table, its caption and its notes kept together                                                                            |
| `bigstrut`       | A taller strut for a cell a rule runs close to                                                                              |

<Callout type="warn">
  `dcolumn`'s `D` column sets numbers centred rather than aligned on the decimal point, and `hhline`'s rules are drawn as one rule across the table.
</Callout>

## Tables from data

`csvsimple` and `datatool` are built in, so a table can be filled from a file of comma-, semicolon- or tab-separated values:

```latex
\usepackage{csvsimple}
\csvautobooktabular{results.csv}
```

`\csvreader` with `head to column names`, `\DTLloaddb`, `\DTLforeach`, `\DTLsort` and `\DTLsumcolumn` are there too, and pgfplotstable's `\pgfplotstabletypeset` sets a table from its file.

## Right to left

A table in Arabic or Hebrew runs right to left: its first column at the right, its rules and padding mirrored, as polyglossia sets it. See [Languages](/docs/writing/languages).
