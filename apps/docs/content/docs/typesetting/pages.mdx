## Paper and margins

### The class's options

The class's options set the paper (`a4paper`, `letterpaper`, `a5paper`, `b5paper`, `legalpaper`, `executivepaper`, and `landscape` to turn it) and the type size. See [Documents and Classes](/docs/typesetting/documents#choosing-a-class).

### geometry

`geometry` sets the rest, as package options or later with `\geometry`:

```latex
\usepackage[a4paper, margin=2cm]{geometry}
\geometry{left=3cm, right=2cm}
```

| Key                         | What it sets                           |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| `paperwidth`, `paperheight` | The paper's size.                      |
| `margin`                    | Every margin.                          |
| `hmargin`, `vmargin`        | The left and right, or top and bottom. |
| `left`, `right`             | One side.                              |
| `top`, `bottom`             | The top or bottom.                     |

A later, more specific key wins over an earlier, more general one, as in LaTeX.

### LaTeX's own lengths

LaTeX's page lengths mean what they mean there too: `\setlength{\topmargin}{...}`, `\oddsidemargin`, `\textwidth`, `\headsep` and `\footskip`. `fullpage` and `a4wide` are accepted.

## Columns

### Two columns

`twocolumn` as a class option sets the whole document in two columns. `\twocolumn[...]` starts two columns with a full-width part above, such as a title and abstract, and `\onecolumn` goes back to one.

### multicol

`multicol`'s `multicols` block sets part of a page in columns:

```latex
\usepackage{multicol}
\begin{multicols}{3}
  Three columns of text, balanced on the last page.
\end{multicols}
```

Columns are balanced on the last page.

### Floats across columns

`figure*` and `table*` span both columns.

## Heads and feet

```latex
\usepackage{fancyhdr}
\usepackage{lastpage}
\pagestyle{fancy}
\fancyhf{}
\fancyhead[L]{\textit{Annual report}}
\fancyhead[R]{\leftmark}
\fancyfoot[C]{Page \thepage\ of \pageref{LastPage}}
```

### Page styles

`\pagestyle{style}` sets every page's head and foot from then on, and `\thispagestyle{style}` only the current page's. LaTeX's styles are `plain`, with the number in the foot, `empty`, with nothing, and `headings`, with running heads.

### fancyhdr

`\pagestyle{fancy}` uses fancyhdr's head and foot. `\fancyhf{}` clears them, and `\fancyhead[L]`, `[C]` and `[R]` and `\fancyfoot` fill each part. fancyhdr's older spellings, `\lhead`, `\chead`, `\rhead`, `\lfoot`, `\cfoot` and `\rfoot`, work too, and `\renewcommand{\headrulewidth}{0pt}` removes the rule under the head.

### Other packages

KOMA-Script's `scrlayer-scrpage` and `titleps` set heads and feet too.

### Running heads

`\markboth{left}{right}` and `\markright{right}` set what `\leftmark` and `\rightmark` show, on that page and the pages after it until another is set.

### Page numbers

`\pagenumbering{roman}` switches to roman numbers (`arabic`, `roman`, `Roman`, `alph` and `Alph`), starting again at one. `\setcounter{page}{n}` sets the current page's number.

***

## Page breaks

### Breaking pages

`\newpage`, `\clearpage`, `\cleardoublepage` and `\pagebreak` break pages. `\cleardoublepage` breaks as `\clearpage` does, without adding a blank page to reach a right-hand one.

### Filling a page

`\vfill` and `\vspace{\fill}` share out what a page leaves.

### Empty pages

A page holding nothing but a numbering change or a contents entry is never shipped as a blank page.

## The PDF

mLaTeX writes the PDF itself.

### Fonts

Fonts are embedded, cut down to the glyphs the document uses.

### Pictures

JPEGs are embedded unchanged; PNG and WebP are compressed; PDF pages, EPS and SVG pictures are embedded as vectors.

### Links and bookmarks

Links and bookmarks come with `hyperref`; see [References and Links](/docs/typesetting/references).

### Information

`\hypersetup`'s `pdftitle`, `pdfauthor`, `pdfsubject` and `pdfkeywords` are the file's title, author, subject and keywords:

```latex
\usepackage{hyperref}
\hypersetup{pdftitle={Energy Estimates}, pdfauthor={Mara Ellison}}
```

### The same document gives the same file

The PDF's identifier is made from its own bytes, so compiling the same document twice gives the same file.

### PDF/A

For archiving, mLaTeX writes PDF/A-2b when asked, with the document's information as XMP metadata and an sRGB output intent:

```latex
\DocumentMetadata{pdfstandard=A-2b}
\documentclass{article}
```

`pdfx`'s `a-2b` option and its kin do the same.

### No file but the PDF

mLaTeX writes only the PDF: no `.aux`, `.log`, `.toc`, `.out` or `.bbl`. The PDF goes beside the document, or into the folder `--output-directory` names, under the document's name or the one `--job-name` gives. A program embedding mLaTeX can receive the PDF as bytes in memory instead; see [Embedding](/docs/embedding).
